## Hard limits (apply to every marketing task)

1. **Users stay anonymous, always.** No sign-up, email, phone, name or ad ID is collected or asked for in any campaign, form, waitlist or survey.
2. **No health or outcome claims.** Never say or imply the app helps people quit, reduces cravings, improves health, or works for X% of users. Describe what the app *is* and *does* (features), not what it will *do to the user*.

## Product Overview
**App Store name:** Atrest: Quit Vaping
**One-liner:** An iOS app for adults who want to quit vaping, with no account and conversations kept only on the phone.
**What it does (features):**
- **AI Guide** — a chat guide. Optional, see "Data" below.
- **Craving game** — keeps fingers busy during a craving.
- **Virtual walk** — a walk through a fantastical landscape, as a distraction.
- **Journal** — voice and text.
- **Slip tracking** — log slips.
**App Store category:** Health & Fitness. [ASSUMPTION — owner said "Health and Well Being"; Health & Fitness is the closest App Store category.]
**Product type:** Consumer mobile app, iOS only.
**Business model:** Free to download and use. The AI Guide is free for the first 20 turns, then needs a subscription: 12.99 per month or 39.99 per year. The 20 free turns are a one-time lifetime allowance. [ASSUMPTION — prices are in euros.]
**Maker:** One developer, based in Belgium. DSA trader status is set up.

## Data (source for all privacy wording)
- No account.
- AI Guide messages go through the developer's own server to an AI model made by Anthropic and run by Amazon Web Services. Both are in the EU.
- Before the first message, a screen explains this and asks for consent. Nothing is sent until the user taps "Allow and send". It can be turned off in Settings. When it's off, nothing typed in the guide leaves the phone.
- The server keeps no messages. The AI is set to keep nothing. Conversations are stored only on the phone.
- Voice journal audio and transcription stay on the phone.
- [ASSUMPTION — no analytics, crash-reporting or ad SDK in the app.]
- Subscriptions run through Apple. The developer gets no personal payment data.

**How to say it:** describe the facts above. Don't use "zero data" in public copy: messages do leave the phone when the user allows it, so the phrase can be called misleading. Safer: "No account. Your conversations stay on your phone."

## Pricing wording
"Free for life" is risky: the AI Guide becomes paid after 20 turns, and EU consumer rules treat a "free" claim as misleading if the paid part isn't clear. Safer: "Free to use. AI Guide: 20 free messages, then 12.99/month or 39.99/year." Apple also asks that paid parts are disclosed in the listing.

## Rules to check against (before using US-centric advice)
These are areas to check, not legal advice. Each needs verifying against the current source.
- **App Store Review Guidelines** — health/medical claims and accuracy (section 1.4), misleading metadata and screenshots (2.3), privacy and data (5.1), subscriptions (3.1.2). The name, subtitle and keywords can only be changed with a new app version; promotional text can be changed any time.
- **App Store privacy label** — must match the "Data" section above.
- **GDPR and ePrivacy** — no tracking, ad IDs or analytics cookies without consent; the anonymity rule is stricter anyway. US tactics like retargeting pixels, email capture and lookalike audiences are out.
- **EU AI Act** — users must be told they're talking to an AI. The consent screen likely covers this. [ASSUMPTION — verify.]
- **EU Medical Device Regulation (MDR 2017/745)** — software whose stated purpose is to treat or prevent a condition (e.g. nicotine dependence) can count as a medical device. Marketing wording helps define that purpose, and an AI guide on addiction raises the stakes. [ASSUMPTION — needs checking; a key reason for hard limit 2.]
- **EU Unfair Commercial Practices Directive** — no misleading claims, fake urgency, fake reviews, or unclear "free" claims.
- **Rules on e-cigarette advertising (Tobacco Products Directive and Belgian law)** — check whether ads or visuals showing vape products are restricted, even in a quit context. [ASSUMPTION]
- **Ad platforms' own health/addiction policies** (Apple Search Ads, Meta, TikTok, Google) — check before any paid campaign.
